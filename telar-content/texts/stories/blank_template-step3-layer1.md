The skill of being able to interpret and create a quipu was passed down through each  generation, typically a father would teach his son. As mentioned in Garcilaso _ *Posts, relays,and the quipus*_ the quipucamayoc was not an individual role. Garcilaso states that each town would have more than one quipucamayoc depending on the population of the town. The smallest of towns would have a minimum of 4 while bigger towns would have up to over twenty or thirty. With the amount of detail and information that is represented in a quipu, Garcilaso states that it is important to have multiple quipucamayocs to keep the information as true as possible and error free. If an error was found within the quipus, then all or none would be found at fault. Being a quipucamayoc required an excellent memory and understanding of the job. The quipucamayoc had to have the ability to describe what information was being stored and what each detail meant. Garcilaso  states that it was best to keep the practice within the bloodlines as the tradition was better preserved.

Garcilaso - Posts, relays, and the quipus (Royal Commentaries bk 6 chs 7-9, pp. 328-333)
