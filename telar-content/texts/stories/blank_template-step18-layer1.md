     The Codex Mendoza, as with other Mexican Codices, functions thoroughly through community conventions.  As the codices utilize icons and pictorial elements, they rely heavily on oral tradition.  Due to this fact, codices require a sense of communal memory, as the true information of such writing was in the hands of those with the true knowledge to understand the definitive features within the codex.  Moreover, the pictorial elements regarded to in the codex are essentially up to the orators transcription, and such was done in a community setting – invocative of communal entertainment and cultural artistic tradition.  Therefore, the orator, as they were to transcribe the codex with artistic freedom – performed the codex's pages, interpreting and describing the pages in an elaborate, nuanced, and yet also a specific manner.  Overall, codices helped organize a strong sense of community across mesoamerican regions, as they not only visibly exemplified what was to be understood, they relied on a significantly heartful communal understanding developed by oral tradition.
