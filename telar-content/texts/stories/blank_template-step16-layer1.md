     It is notable that this Codex was not created in the specific traditional manner of either deerskin, or indigenous amate bark paper, instead it was created on paper – from the Spanish – by Mexican scribes at the request of Viceroy Antonio de Mendoza, who was the highest ranking colonial figure of 'New Spain' at the time.  Decidingly, the codex entails details regarding some of the history from the region, specifically featuring tales of Aztec conquest.  While demonstrating Aztec imperialism through pictographs of fallen temples, the codex features such detail of even entailing the processes that maintained such imperial subordination.  For example, pages 17v to 54v detail various tributes to the Aztec ruler, Motecuhzoma Xocoyotzin, given from the 26 towns in which the Aztecs had forcibly overcome.  Culturally significant objects such as cloths, capes, warrior costumes, shields, beans, maize, chia, huautli, and more – all of which was to be in tribute either once or twice a year.
