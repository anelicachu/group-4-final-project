     Mexican Codices challenge current comprehensions concerning writing, as they function strongly through pictorial representations, as contrasted with phoneticism.  Due to strong eurocentric ideologies that carry academic thought, there can come a sense of strong connotations for defining writing.  Therefore, because of the absence of phoneticism, except for perhaps in the context of Mayan signification of concept-sounds, understanding Mexican Codices as writing contradicts dominant definitions of writing and its definitive constructs.  The Mexicas, Mixtecs, and Maya are those that specifically rely on codices to relay and maintain community historicism.   Due to the lack of phoneticism, interpretations are variable and essentially personal, but still with special regard to cultural conventions.  Codices can contain a comprehensive collection of features that resemble significant historical events, tribute lists, or even a map.  Overall, codices are the main documents that dictate pre-colonial culture of Mesoamerica, and they feature a complete absence of phonological comprehension, instead relying hollistically on pictorial and iconic representations, based on communal conventions and understandings.
