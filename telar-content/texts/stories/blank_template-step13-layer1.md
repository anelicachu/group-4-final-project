---

This transcultural project was intended to work as an encyclopedia of all Indigenous knowledge and history, gathered throughout decades of research. It was a collaborative effort between Bernardino de Sahagún and an educated team of Christian indigenous scholars that produced the majority of the content in the encyclopedia that was Nahua-focused. It should be understood to have two purposes: to document indigenous culture and use it to convert them to Christianity. It was also written during an impactful time of "chaos of conquest, disease, and consolidation of imperial power in Mexico" (Terraciano, 2019, p. 2). This is relevant because the current events bring shape and purpose to the process of their writing. At the time, disease was heavily impacting the indigenous population, which influenced the way in which the Nahua collaborators processed their culture on paper through their writing. The project continued to undergo obstacles, as the project was read as "promoting" indigenous culture. Policies issued by King Phillip II prohibiting writing about indigenous culture further rushed the team to complete their manuscripts, leaving an unfinished product.
