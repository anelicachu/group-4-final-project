The quipu was a form of "writing" used by the Inca in order to record numeric data. To this day, it is unknown what the numbers represent, but it is believed to represent data such as births, deaths, crops, etc. Each characteristic of a quipu represents different information. Things such as the placement of the knots, the amount of knots, the amount of ropes, the way the rope was tied, the length of the quipu and the rope, the color of the ropes, etc. The quipu would have a knot on one end and the other would be a free end. This rope was the primary cord while the cords attached directly to this cord were the pendant cords. The pendant cords could also have more cords attached to them which were the subsidiary cords. It seems the Incas record keeping system was extremely detailed and specific. As shown in the pictured quipu, some knots of different colors are weaved together, while others are one single strand and a singular color. This quipu was one of  the backbones to recordkeeping in Incan culture.
